AI chatbots have gone from novelty to near-default expectation on many websites. But not every business needs one — and a poorly built chatbot can do more harm than good. Here's how to think about it honestly.

## When a chatbot genuinely helps

A well-built AI chatbot earns its place when:

- You get the same handful of questions repeatedly (hours, pricing, availability, process)
- Leads arrive outside business hours and currently just... wait
- Your team spends real time on repetitive, low-complexity support requests
- You want to qualify leads before they reach a human

In these cases, a chatbot trained specifically on your business content can resolve a meaningful percentage of inquiries instantly, freeing your team to focus on higher-value conversations.

## When it's not worth it (yet)

If your business gets low website traffic, or your customer questions are highly varied and require real judgment, a chatbot may not be worth the investment yet. A bad chatbot experience — one that gives wrong answers or traps visitors in a loop — actively damages trust.

## The difference between a good and bad implementation

The quality gap between chatbot implementations is enormous. A good one is:

- Trained specifically on your actual business content, not generic web knowledge
- Honest about what it doesn't know, with a clean handoff to a human
- Fast, and doesn't feel like talking to a wall

A bad one hallucinates answers, frustrates visitors, and quietly damages the trust you've built with your brand.

## The real ROI

The businesses that get the most value from AI chatbots aren't chasing a trend — they're solving a specific, measurable problem: too many repetitive questions, too little coverage outside business hours, or too much time spent qualifying unqualified leads.

If that sounds like your business, a chatbot is worth exploring. If it doesn't, your money is better spent elsewhere for now.
