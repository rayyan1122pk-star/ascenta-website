SEO gets treated like a mysterious black box, but the fundamentals are actually straightforward. Understanding them helps you ask better questions — whether you're working with a developer, an agency, or handling it yourself.

## Technical SEO comes first

Before you worry about keywords or content, your website needs to be technically sound:

- Fast load times
- Mobile-friendly on every page
- Clean URL structure
- Proper metadata (titles and descriptions) on every page
- A sitemap Google can actually crawl

If any of these are broken, great content won't save you. Search engines need to be able to access, understand, and trust your site before they'll rank it.

## Structured data matters more than ever

Structured data (JSON-LD) tells search engines exactly what your content is — a business, a product, an article, a review. This is increasingly important as AI-powered search tools like Google's AI Overviews rely on structured signals to understand and summarize content accurately.

## Content should answer real questions

Search engines reward content that genuinely answers what people are searching for. That means writing for your actual customers first, not stuffing pages with keywords. A clear, well-organized page that answers a real question will consistently outperform a keyword-stuffed page that doesn't.

## Local SEO is often the highest-leverage move

If you serve a specific area — a clinic, a law firm, a contractor — local SEO is usually your biggest opportunity. A properly configured Google Business Profile, consistent business information across the web, and location-specific content can outperform generic national SEO efforts by a wide margin.

## The bottom line

SEO isn't magic, and it isn't instant. It's the compounding result of a technically sound website, genuinely useful content, and consistency over time. Get the fundamentals right, and the rest becomes much easier.
