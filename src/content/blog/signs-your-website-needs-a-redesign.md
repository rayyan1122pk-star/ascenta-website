Deciding when to redesign a website is a hard call. Change too often and you waste money; wait too long and you're actively losing business. Here are the signals that consistently mean it's time.

## 1. It doesn't work well on mobile

If your site was built more than a few years ago and mobile feels like an afterthought, you're likely losing the majority of your traffic — most web traffic today is mobile.

## 2. It takes more than 3 seconds to load

We covered the cost of slow load times in a previous post — if your site is sluggish, that alone is reason enough to prioritize a rebuild.

## 3. You can't update it yourself

If every small content change requires a developer, your website is quietly costing you time and money every single month.

## 4. Your competitors' sites look noticeably better

This one is subjective, but it matters. If a prospective client compares your site to a competitor's and yours looks dated, that perception directly affects trust — even if your service is better.

## 5. Your conversion rate is low despite decent traffic

Traffic without conversions usually points to a design or messaging problem, not a marketing problem. If people are visiting but not converting, the site itself is often the bottleneck.

## 6. It's not built on a modern, secure foundation

Old platforms accumulate security risk and technical debt over time. If your site runs on outdated technology, a rebuild is often cheaper long-term than continuing to patch it.

## 7. It doesn't reflect who you are today

Businesses evolve. If your website still reflects an earlier, smaller version of your company, it's holding back the growth you've already achieved elsewhere.

## What to do next

If two or more of these apply to your website, it's worth a conversation. A redesign doesn't have to mean starting from zero — often it means rebuilding with intent, keeping what works, and fixing what doesn't.
